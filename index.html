Race, income and wealth in New York City

Income vs. wealth: a two-tier gap

A white household in New York City earns roughly 1.8 times what a Black household earns. The same white household holds more than 100 times the wealth of a Black household — and Latino households, on average, hold none at all.

Methodology

Source: Pascale Mevs Fitzgerald for Vital City. Income figures: U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey, 2023 one-year estimates, median household income for non-Hispanic white and non-Hispanic Black households in New York City. Wealth figures: New School Institute on Race, Power and Political Economy / Federal Reserve Bank of Boston "Color of Wealth" research applied to New York City, citing median household net worth by race and ethnicity. Homeownership rates: American Community Survey, latest one-year estimates, owner-occupied housing tenure by householder race/ethnicity.

How to read this: Income is what a household earns in a year. Wealth is what a household owns (home equity, savings, investments) minus what it owes (mortgages, student loans, credit-card debt). Latino median wealth is reported as effectively zero in the cited research — when debts roughly equal assets — and is shown here as a "no bar" indicator rather than a literal $0 point estimate.

Limitations: Wealth medians are highly sensitive to sample size and methodology; figures pool across age, immigration status and household composition. Income data are from a single year; wealth and homeownership shift more slowly.
